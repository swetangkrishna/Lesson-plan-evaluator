Objective (5 min)

By the end of the session, students will (a) define AI literacy in their own words and (b) identify at least three distinct, industry-spanning AI use cases (e.g., healthcare, finance, manufacturing), noting benefits, risks, and stakeholders.

Introduction (10 min)

Hook (think–pair–share): “Where did an AI system touch your day today?”
Collect 5–7 examples (recommendations, spam filters, translation, autocomplete) on the board.

Mini-poll prompts:
	•	“AI literacy is only about coding.” (True/False)
	•	“Large language models always produce reliable facts.” (True/False)

Bridge: Explain that AI literacy is broader than tool use—it blends conceptual understanding, critical evaluation, ethics, and communication.

Instruction (30 min)

1) Working definition (write on slide/board):
AI literacy is the ability to understand what AI systems are and how they work at a high level; use them appropriately; critically evaluate their outputs and impacts; and communicate decisions about them—including ethical, legal, and societal implications.

2) Core components (with brief examples):
	•	Conceptual knowledge: prediction vs. generation; supervised/unsupervised learning; “hallucinations”; data drift.
	•	Data literacy: datasets, labeling, bias, privacy, provenance, consent.
	•	Tool proficiency: prompts, parameters, constraints; when not to use AI.
	•	Evaluation & risk: accuracy vs. recall/precision; robustness; transparency; auditability.
	•	Ethics & governance: fairness, explainability, accountability, IP, safety, regulation.
	•	Human–AI collaboration: roles, oversight, escalation paths.

3) Anchor use cases (show 1–2 slides with concise vignettes):
	•	Healthcare (diagnostic support): Model flags abnormalities in chest X-rays for radiologist review. Benefits: throughput, earlier detection. Risks: bias across populations, liability, dataset shift.
	•	Finance (fraud detection): Transaction anomaly detection. Benefits: reduced losses. Risks: false positives harming customers, adversarial adaptation.
	•	Manufacturing (predictive maintenance): Sensor data forecasts equipment failure. Benefits: less downtime. Risks: poor sensor quality, overfitting to specific machines.

(Optionally mention others: education—adaptive learning; logistics—route optimization; agriculture—yield prediction; law—document review; energy—demand forecasting.)

Practice (25 min)

Activity: Industry Jigsaw (teams of 3–4)
	1.	Assign each team an industry (healthcare, finance, manufacturing, education, logistics, agriculture, marketing, public sector).
	2.	Provide a one-page scenario per team (or let them draft one in 5 minutes).
	3.	Teams complete a Use-Case Canvas (template):
	•	Problem & stakeholders
	•	AI approach (classification, NLP, forecasting, vision, RL; data needed)
	•	Benefits/ROI & success metrics (e.g., precision/recall, time saved, cost avoided)
	•	Risks & mitigations (bias, privacy, security, misuse, human-in-the-loop)
	•	Deployment plan (pilot, evaluation, monitoring, rollback)
	4.	Gallery walk (optional, 8 min): Each team posts their canvas; peers leave 1 sticky note: one strength, one risk to watch.

Instructor support: circulate, probe for metrics (“How would you know it works?”), risks (“Who could be harmed?”), and governance (“Who signs off?”).

Assessment (5–10 min)

Quick Check (individual, submit):
	1.	Define AI literacy in one or two sentences.
	2.	List three distinct AI use cases across different industries and name for each: one benefit, one risk, and one metric you’d track.

Rubric (5 pts total):
	•	Clear, comprehensive definition touching on understand–use–evaluate–communicate (2 pts)
	•	Three industry-distinct use cases (1 pt)
	•	For each: plausible benefit, relevant risk, and measurable metric (2 pts)

Exit prompt (optional): “When would you choose not to use AI for this problem, and why?”

⸻

Materials: slides or board, polling tool or show of hands, one-page scenarios, Use-Case Canvas handout, sticky notes.
Notes for instructor: Aim for a 75-minute class. If 50 minutes, trim gallery walk and limit teams to 5 minutes prep.
