Decode AI: Real-World Applications Today

Learning Objectives
By the end of the lesson, students should be able to:
Define AI literacy in their own words and explain its importance in professional and civic contexts.
Identify and describe at least three distinct real-world use cases of AI across different industries.
Evaluate ethical and practical implications of a chosen AI use case and propose one recommendation for responsible use.
Vocabulary
Artificial Intelligence (AI) - Machines performing tasks that require human-like intelligence.
Machine Learning - Algorithms that learn patterns from data to make predictions.
Model - A mathematical or computational representation used to make predictions.
Training Data - Examples used to teach a model how to perform a task.
Supervised Learning - Learning from labeled examples to map inputs to outputs.
Unsupervised Learning - Finding patterns in unlabeled data.
Natural Language Processing (NLP) - Techniques for computers to understand human language.
Bias - Systematic error in data or models that leads to unfair outcomes.
Automation - Using technology to perform tasks without continuous human input.
Explainability - How well a model’s decisions can be interpreted by humans.
Essential Questions
How does understanding AI change the way we evaluate products and services in different industries?
What responsibilities do organizations and individuals have when deploying AI systems that affect people?
How can we balance the benefits of AI-driven automation with risks like bias, privacy loss, or job disruption?
Lesson Procedure
Opening Hook

Student-facing activity:
Think of one app, website, or device you used today that might use AI. Write its name and one way it might be using AI.
Teacher guidance:
Pose the prompt publicly and allow 2-3 volunteers to share examples.
Record 6-8 examples on the board spanning different sectors (healthcare, finance, education, retail, transportation, media).
Use this list to link to later examples and spark interest.
Direct Instruction

Student-facing content:
Short definitions: AI literacy, machine learning, model, training data, bias, explainability.
Present three industry use cases with concrete, specific details:
Healthcare - diagnostic imaging assistance: models trained on medical images to flag anomalies, support clinician decision-making, speed triage.
Finance - fraud detection: real-time transaction monitoring models that identify anomalous patterns to block or flag suspicious transactions.
Retail/Customer Experience - recommendation systems: models that analyze past purchases and browsing to personalize product suggestions.
Additional brief examples: autonomous vehicles (transportation), automated résumé screening (HR), chatbots for customer service (services), predictive maintenance (manufacturing).
Teacher guidance:
Use short, concrete stories for each use case (for example, describe a patient pathway where AI helps prioritize urgent scans).
Display one slide or whiteboard diagram per use case showing Inputs - Model - Outputs - Human role.
Emphasize limits: models need quality training data, can reflect biases, and require human oversight.
Guided Practice

Student-facing activity:
In small groups (3-4), students pick one use case from the Direct Instruction list.
Each group completes the following 3-step worksheet together:
Describe how AI is used - inputs, model behavior, outputs.
List two benefits for users/organizations.
Identify two potential risks or ethical concerns and one way to mitigate each.
Teacher guidance:
Circulate, listen, and prompt groups with probing questions: "What data does the model need? Who provides the data? Who is affected by errors?"
After 12-18 minutes, invite 3 groups to present a 2-minute summary of their answers.
Provide corrective feedback and highlight strong mitigation strategies (data audits, human-in-the-loop, transparency notices).
Independent Practice

Student-facing task:
Individually, students write a one-page brief (approx. 300-400 words) that:
Defines AI literacy in their own words.
Describes one chosen AI use case (different from the group’s if possible), including inputs, stakeholders, and one concrete recommendation to improve responsible use.
The brief must include at least one ethical implication and a specific mitigation recommendation.
Teacher guidance:
Provide a simple rubric: clear definition (30%), accurate description of use case (40%), viable recommendation and ethical consideration (30%).
Allow class time to begin the brief and ask students to finish as homework if needed.
Closing

Student-facing reflection:
Post one-sentence exit ticket: What is one new thing you learned about AI use cases and one question you still have?
Teacher guidance:
Collect exit tickets as students leave or submit digitally.
Read 6-8 exit tickets quickly to adjust next lesson’s focus on misconceptions or unanswered questions.
Extension Activities & Assessments
Differentiated Activities
Below Grade Level (Support):
Provide a completed example brief and ask students to highlight the use case elements and mitigation steps.
Pair with a stronger peer for a scaffolded group worksheet where roles are explicit (recorder, reporter, researcher).
At Grade Level (Core):
Complete the independent one-page brief using rubric, plus a short peer review exchange with two classmates providing feedback.
Prepare a 3-minute in-class presentation summarizing the brief and recommended mitigation.
Above Grade Level (Extension):
Conduct a short mini-research: find a recent news article about an AI deployment, analyze the technical and ethical elements, and write a 2-page critique with citations.
Design a simple conceptual flowchart or pseudocode for how a chosen AI system could incorporate explainability or fairness checks.
Assessments
Formative:
Group worksheet review during Guided Practice - teacher notes on understanding of inputs/outputs and ethical awareness.
Exit ticket summarizing one new learning and one question - quick check for misconceptions and engagement.
Summative:
Individual one-page brief graded with rubric measuring the three objectives: definition of AI literacy, description of at least three industry attributes via chosen use case, and a recommendation addressing ethical/practical implications.
Optional extended summative - 5-minute oral presentation of brief with Q&A to assess depth of reasoning and communication.
Reflections
Did students produce clear, distinct definitions of AI literacy and demonstrate meaningful differences between the use cases?
Which groups struggled with identifying real risks or mitigation strategies, and what scaffolds would help next time?
How effectively did the lesson connect technical descriptions to ethical implications - should more concrete examples or data visualizations be added?
Homework
Find one real news item or company blog describing an AI system. Write a 200-word summary stating the industry, the AI functionality, one potential benefit, and one risk.
Optional - create a one-slide visual (single image or diagram) that shows Inputs - Model - Outputs - Human role for the system you summarized.
